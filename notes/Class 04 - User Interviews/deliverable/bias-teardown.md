# Bias Teardown: Ten Interview Questions for Zepto

Ten questions a PM might realistically draft to research why users churn or stay loyal on Zepto. Each one carries at least one of the six named biases, followed by a rewrite that passes the what-and-how test.

---

**1. "Do you think Zepto's 10-minute delivery promise is what makes you choose it over other apps?"**
**Bias:** Leading (positive framing) — the question hands the user the answer.
**Rewrite:** "What made you open Zepto the last time you needed something quickly, and how did you decide between that and other options?"

---

**2. "Would you use a subscription plan if it guaranteed faster delivery?"**
**Bias:** Assumptive/solution bias, future intention — the feature is embedded, and it asks the user to predict behaviour.
**Rewrite:** "What are you currently doing to get things delivered faster, and are you paying anything extra for that?"

---

**3. "Zepto's prices are higher than local stores, right? Does that bother you?"**
**Bias:** Leading (negative framing) — the premise is stated as fact before the question is even asked.
**Rewrite:** "Walk me through the last time you compared prices between Zepto and somewhere else. What did you notice, and what did you end up doing?"

---

**4. "Do you think adding a loyalty points system would make you order more often?"**
**Bias:** Solution bias, future intention — the answer is a guess about a feature that doesn't exist.
**Rewrite:** "What are the last three things you ordered on Zepto, and what made you open the app each of those times?"

---

**5. "How often do you use Zepto — daily, weekly, or monthly?"**
**Bias:** Data you already own — this sits in Zepto's own analytics.
**Rewrite:** Delete the question. Pull frequency from usage data and spend the interview slot on something the logs can't tell you.

---

**6. "Don't you think the substitution feature, where Zepto swaps an out-of-stock item, is annoying?"**
**Bias:** Leading (negative framing) plus assumptive — the verdict is built into the question.
**Rewrite:** "Tell me about the last time an item you wanted wasn't available. What happened, and how did you handle it?"

---

**7. "As someone who values convenience, do you think Zepto delivers a premium experience?"**
**Bias:** Social desirability plus leading — "as someone who values convenience" flatters the respondent into agreeing.
**Rewrite:** "What does 'a good delivery experience' look like to you, based on any app you've used recently?"

---

**8. "Would you recommend Zepto to a friend who is skeptical about quick commerce?"**
**Bias:** Hypothetical, future intention — asks the user to imagine a scenario rather than report on a real one.
**Rewrite:** "Have you told anyone about Zepto recently? What did you say, and what made you bring it up?"

---

**9. "Does Zepto's app feel faster and more reliable than Blinkit or Instamart?"**
**Bias:** Double-barrelled (two qualities in one question) plus comparison bias — it names competitors and asks for a verdict rather than a story.
**Rewrite:** "What's the last app, other than Zepto, that you used for something similar? What worked well about it?"

---

**10. "If Zepto added same-day returns, would that make you trust the platform more?"**
**Bias:** Solution bias, hypothetical, future intention — three biases stacked into one leading question about an unbuilt feature.
**Rewrite:** "Tell me about the last time you needed to return or report a problem with a Zepto order. What did you do, and how did that go?"

---

## What this taught me

Eight of the ten questions I first drafted carried a bias without my noticing until I ran them against the six-name checklist. The two genuine offenders were #5 (data I'd already own) and #9 (comparison bias, not covered by the class's six but the same underlying flaw — asking for a verdict instead of a story). Every rewrite follows the same move: replace an opinion or a prediction with a specific past event, and strip out any noun that names a feature I want to validate.

The pattern that repeats across almost every bad question: I already had an answer in mind, and the question was built to confirm it rather than test it.
